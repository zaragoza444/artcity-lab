1. Executive Directive and Product Decision
Art City AI Vertical Drama IP Factory is a specialized production operating environment for developing, producing, localizing, mastering and commercializing short-form episodic vertical drama IP. It must not be implemented as a single “generate video” page. The product is a persistent story-and-production system in which characters, canon, episodes, shots, assets, rights, versions, approvals, costs and performance data remain connected over time.
The product must create persistent IP, not disposable generations.
AI proposes and accelerates; human users approve canon, rights, episode lock and final masters.
Every expensive generation must be job-tracked, costed, versioned and associated with a Project/Series/Episode/Shot.
Character, voice, wardrobe, location and style consistency are core product functions, not optional prompt tips.
The system must support AI-native, hybrid live-action and conventional-production-assisted vertical drama.
Kurdish Sorani is a first-class writing, UI, subtitle, dubbing and metadata language.
Provider/model names must be abstracted behind Art City model/workflow labels and the Art City AI Router.
1.1 Recommended product position
Public/product name: Art City AI Vertical Drama IP Factory. Short UI label: AC Vertical Drama. Product promise: “Create a series. Build an IP. Produce episode by episode.” The word “IP” should remain visible because the differentiation is not merely fast vertical video generation; it is persistent franchise development and controlled production.

2. Separation Strategy: Separate Domain, Shared Core
The product should be “separate where specialization creates value, shared where duplication creates risk.” Vertical drama requires season/episode/canon/continuity logic that does not belong inside the generic Studio interface. At the same time, duplicating user accounts, projects, assets, AI credentials, billing and QC would create data fragmentation and unnecessary engineering cost.

18. AI Casting, Voice and Dialogue Production
Voice is part of character identity. Separate fictional Character, performer/cast record and generated VoiceProfile. This supports real actors, synthetic voices, dubbing and recasting without destroying character canon.
18.1 Voice rules
Never expose provider voice IDs as the only canonical identifier.
Require explicit rights/consent when cloning or imitating a real person’s voice.
Keep original dialogue and each translated/dubbed version separately.
Support pronunciation dictionaries for Kurdish names, locations and culturally specific terms.
Allow user to select one approved voice take per cue and preserve alternates.

20. Audio, Music and Sound Design
Audio should be generated/managed as separate tracks so localization and later mastering are possible. A flattened video with permanently baked dialogue/music is acceptable only as a user-selected low-control mode.
20.1 Music safety and rights
Generated or licensed music must retain source/provider/license terms in the rights/provenance ledger. The platform must not label music “copyright free” merely because an AI model generated it. Delivery readiness should show missing/unclear music rights as a blocker or warning according to policy.
21. Assembly, Edit and Versioning
Vertical Drama should orchestrate episode assembly but should reuse Art City Edit for timeline operations and finishing. The Vertical Drama module owns episode/shot order, selected takes and narrative status; Edit owns timeline-level manipulation, captions, color/finishing and export operations.

21.2 Version rules
Every episode cut has an immutable version identifier.
Review comments bind to version + timecode/frame.
Picture lock is a controlled approval state, not a filename convention.
A post-lock change creates a new cut version and invalidates downstream QC/master status until re-approved.
Never delete approved masters automatically when a new version is created.
22. Localization, Dubbing and Cultural Adaptation
Localization is more than translation. The system must support direct translation, dubbed version, subtitle version and licensed cultural remake as distinct derivative types with lineage back to the source episode/IP.

Correct RTL subtitles and UI.
Mixed Kurdish/English names and numbers must not visually reorder.
Pronunciation dictionaries for TTS/dubbing.
Human linguistic approval before master delivery.
Preserve original authored Kurdish text; search normalization is separate.

25. Delivery / Publishing Package Builder
Delivery profiles must be configuration-driven because platform requirements change. Do not hard-code TikTok/Instagram/YouTube/dedicated microdrama specifications into business logic. Admin should be able to create/update delivery profiles with version history.
25.1 Delivery readiness
Show a readiness percentage only as a summary. Always display concrete missing/failed requirements. A score must never hide a blocker such as missing master, failed audio layout, missing rights, rejected subtitles or unresolved QC.
26. Analytics and Performance Learning Loop
Analytics turns the factory into a learning system, but it must not rewrite existing canon automatically. Performance data may come from connected platform APIs, CSV import or manual entry depending on authorization and availability.
26.1 Performance analyst output
Identify patterns with confidence/limitations, not causal certainty.
Compare episodes within the same series and comparable cohorts.
Suggest future episode pacing/hook experiments.
Never alter locked episodes automatically.
Keep a record of recommendation → user decision → later outcome for learning.
Do not build manipulative dark-pattern viewer mechanics into the production tool.
27. AI Agents and Orchestration Rules
“Agents” should be implemented as specialized orchestration roles over structured state, not as independent chatbots with uncontrolled memory. Every agent reads approved/current domain objects, writes a proposed structured result, and passes through validation/approval rules.
27.1 Agent constraints
All agent inputs include explicit workspace/project/IP/season/episode context.
All structured outputs validated by JSON Schema/Pydantic.
Prompt templates are versioned and administrator-controlled.
Agents cannot approve their own output unless a product policy explicitly defines a low-risk auto-approval path.
Agents never bypass rights status, locked canon or spending limits.
Use retrieval from Art City’s canonical objects; do not rely on conversational memory for production truth.

29. Job Queue, State Machine and Reliability
Use Redis-backed durable job orchestration/worker system or equivalent.
Every expensive AI operation is asynchronous and resumable.
Browser refresh must not lose job state.
Use idempotency keys to prevent double-generation/double-charge.
Separate normalized ProductJob from ProviderJob so provider retry/fallback does not create duplicate user-visible work.
Support cancellation when provider allows; billing follows actual provider policy.
Store error category, provider error, retry count, fallback decision and final status.
Limit parallel generation by plan/workspace/project to control spend.
Bulk season production is a parent BatchJob containing per-shot/per-cue child jobs.
Do not mark an episode complete merely because all provider jobs returned; require selection/approval/master states.

30. Data Model and Suggested Database Tables
Use PostgreSQL. The exact ORM is implementation choice, but the relationships and tenant boundaries below should remain. Every domain table must contain or be securely joinable to workspace_id/project_id for authorization and cost attribution.
30.1 Critical database rules
Foreign keys wherever practical; do not rely on arbitrary JSON for core relationships.
JSONB is acceptable for provider-specific settings, flexible metadata and snapshot payloads, but not as a substitute for canonical entities.
Approved versions are immutable; edits create new version rows.
Soft-delete/archive production objects when history must be retained.
All money uses Decimal/numeric, never binary float.
Store timestamps in UTC; render in user/project timezone.
Store locale/language as BCP 47 or controlled language tag fields.
Normalize search indexes without destructively modifying display text.

Appendix C — Minimal FastAPI Generation Contract
@router.post('/shots/{shot_id}/generate')
async def generate_shot(
    shot_id: UUID,
    req: ShotGenerateRequest,
    user=Depends(require_permission('vertical_drama.shot.generate')),
):
    shot = await shot_service.get_authorized(shot_id, user)
    await rights_policy.assert_generation_allowed(shot)
    await budget_policy.assert_within_limit(shot.project_id, req.max_cost_usd)

    normalized = await production_service.build_generation_request(
        shot=shot,
        user=user,
        request=req,
    )
    job = await ai_router.queue(normalized, idempotency_key=req.idempotency_key)
    return JobResponse.model_validate(job)

Appendix F — Prompt Engineering Rules
Prompt templates are server-side and versioned.
Use explicit locale/output_language.
Inject only necessary approved canon/context, not the entire workspace indiscriminately.
Use IDs/reference labels so generated output can map back to canonical objects.
Require structured output schemas for production data.
Separate creative instruction from safety/rights/provider constraints.
Never ask the model to calculate authoritative budget or rights status.
Store prompt_template_version and input-object hashes in job lineage.
Use Sorani examples in ckb-IQ templates and regression-test them.
Do not place provider secrets or private signed URLs into model prompts unless technically required and approved.
Appendix G — Definition of Done for a Mastered Episode
IP and rights policy allow production/delivery.

All final shots selected/approved.

Episode metadata/number/title/language correct.
Manifest/checksum generated.

Risk / decision | Why it matters | Mitigation / required decision
Treating product as generator | No defensible workflow/IP value; continuity collapses. | Ship IP/bible/episode/canon/identity workflow as core.
Character drift | Breaks audience trust and serial continuity. | Reference locks, appearance variants, provider adapters, QA.
Provider lock-in | Prices/models/availability change. | Shared Router + provider abstraction + registry.
AI cost explosion | Large seasons multiply failed candidates. | Preflight cost, batch caps, acceptance-rate metrics, private GPU where economic.
Rights ambiguity | Can block distribution/partnerships. | IP Vault, source declaration, releases/licenses, human review.
AI authorship uncertainty | Jurisdiction-specific protection may depend on human contribution. | Contribution ledger, human approvals/revisions; legal review outside product.
Canon drift | Batch LLM generation invents contradictions. | Approved snapshots, structured canon facts, continuity checks.
Over-automation | Mass generation before creative approval wastes money. | Approval gates before expensive stages.
Kurdish treated late | RTL/script/TTS issues become architectural rework. | ckb-IQ from Phase 0 with golden fixtures.
Analytics overclaim | Correlation can be misrepresented as creative certainty. | Confidence/limitations; experiment recommendations only.
Separate platform duplication | Splits assets/accounts/billing and increases maintenance. | Separate domain, shared Art City core.
Monolithic coupling | Future scale difficult if domain boundaries are unclear. | Modular package/interfaces/event contracts; extract services later only when justified.
